\begin{abstract}
Comparative research routinely reads a single wave-pair mean contrast as a
persistent, distribution-wide, country-level trend---attaching uncertainty to the
wrong unit and over-detecting change. We give a deployable instrument: a
finite-sample simultaneous band over a country's whole attitude \emph{trajectory},
with the country as the exchangeable unit and a hierarchy of claims whose top rung is
the honest object. The band is a clustered conformal band exact at any $K$, inside an
adaptive selector whose validity is selection-free---its fallback branches are
nested, so at survey scale the deployed guarantee is exact under \emph{any}
selection rule---and which corrects for survey design only at the many-unit scale
where that correction is provably reliable. In the ESS (rounds 9--11), net decline
in parliamentary trust is certified in six of thirty countries and persistent
decline in one (Greece, on a single wave pair)---not the twenty flagged marginally.
On the World Values Survey (1981--2022), a marginal wave-pair reading over-counts
the persistent phenomenon by $2.6$--$6.5\times$, and what survives concentrates in
post-communist and Arab-Spring-aftermath states, not the consolidated West. A
subtler second error---the calibration objects are themselves
estimated from complex samples---we resolve by scope: the correction is
non-identified without the design-noise law and, for the deployed procedure,
unreachable at survey scale, so abstention is principled.
\end{abstract}
