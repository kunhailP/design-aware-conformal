\begin{abstract}
Repeated cross-national surveys are used to make claims about whole
trajectories of national response distributions, while uncertainty is computed
for single wave contrasts; and when country trajectories calibrate
cross-country prediction, the calibration objects are themselves estimates
from complex samples. We develop simultaneous inference that attaches
uncertainty to both units. Within a country, one design-based band certifies a
partially ordered family of trajectory claims --- from a single wave pair to
persistent, distribution-wide decline --- with no further correction. Across
countries, a clustered conformal band gives finite-sample coverage for
survey-estimated trajectories; we prove that correcting toward the latent
trajectory is unidentified without design-noise information, and characterize
when the correction is unnecessary, unlearnable, or feasible --- infeasible at
cross-national scale, active at the small-area unit of the same survey. In the
European Social Survey, a marginal reading flags twenty of thirty countries;
six certify net decline over 2018--2024. Over 2002--2024 none certifies a
persistent slide, a claim only crisis-scale change clears, while closed
testing certifies at least six true span decliners among thirty-three.
The same discipline cuts the certified WVS/EVS deconsolidation set
severalfold, toward post-communist and Arab-Spring states.
\end{abstract}
