\begin{abstract}
Comparative research routinely reads one wave-pair contrast as a
persistent, distribution-wide trend---attaching uncertainty to the wrong unit
and over-detecting change. We give a deployable instrument: a finite-sample
simultaneous band over a country's whole attitude \emph{trajectory}, with the
country as the exchangeable unit and a graded family of claims---finite-sample
valid at any $K$ for the estimated trajectory, explicitly bounded for the latent one,
inside a selector whose nested fallbacks preserve it under
any selection rule. In the ESS the hierarchy cuts a marginal
reading of twenty of thirty countries to net decline in six. Over the full
2002--2024 record none certifies a persistent slide, a rung only crisis-scale
change clears; reading every ordered contrast and both
directions off one band, twenty-three of thirty-three countries certify both a
fall and a rise, and span erosion certifies in eight. On the World Values Survey the same shift cuts the certified set
$1.9$--$4.8\times$; what survives is post-communist and Arab-Spring states. The second error---calibration on estimated objects---we
resolve by scope: the correction is non-identified without the design-noise law
and unreachable across countries, but at the small-area unit of the same survey
the frozen selector activates it, cutting the fallback band by a certified
$16$--$22\%$ without losing coverage.
\end{abstract}
