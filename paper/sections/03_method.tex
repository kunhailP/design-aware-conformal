\section{Simultaneous inference for both units}\label{sec:method}

The two wrong units get two procedures: within-country certification of a
whole claim family, and cross-country transport with a correction that
activates only where it is provably reliable.

\subsection{Within-country certification: one band, a family of claims}
\label{sec:claims}

Write
$\theta_{a,b}(t)=F_b(t)-F_a(t)$ for the contrast between observed rounds
$a<b$ at threshold $t$, positive where response mass has moved below $t$, a
decline. Let $\mathcal T_0$ be the
preregistered core of thresholds. Table~\ref{tab:claims} defines each claim
as an event for this contrast surface. The claims form a partial order, not
a chain. Persistent implies both net and any-pair, but net and any-pair are
incomparable: a recovered dip certifies an adjacent pair without net
decline, and a slow accumulation certifies a span while no adjacent pair
clears the critical value. Nor are the claims separate tests: each claim,
every sub-span, and the reverse direction is a function of one object, the
country's surface of wave-pair contrasts, so all are certified on one
coverage event.

\begin{proposition}[Claim-family transfer]\label{prop:transfer}
Let $\widehat B$ be a simultaneous $1-\alpha$ band for a country's contrast
surface $\theta=\{\theta_{a, b}(t)\}$ over every ordered pair of observed rounds,
and let $\{A_j\}$ be any family of claims, each a set of surfaces. Define
the certified subfamily $J=\{j: \widehat B\subseteq A_j\}$. Then
$\Pr\big(\theta\in A_j\ \text{for every}\ j\in J\big)\ge1-\alpha$, with no
dependence on $|J|$: on $\{\theta\in\widehat B\}$, set inclusion delivers every
certified claim at once.
\end{proposition}

Reading each deterministic consequence off one simultaneous region is the post-hoc logic of \citet{scheffe1953method},
of closed testing \citep{marcus1976closed}, and of the selection-free bounds
of \citet{goeman2011multiple,katsevich2020simultaneous}. The contribution is
the deployment: trend claims in this literature are made claim by claim,
whereas one studentized sup-$t$ band over the whole contrast family removes
the corrections a per-claim construction needs (no Bonferroni across a
country's pairs, no budget for certified recoveries, no endpoint-selection
cost) at the price of a wider critical value, reported in
Section~\ref{sec:political}. The persistent object is one functional band
\citep{diquigiovanni2021conformal}, an estimand distinction rather than an
error-rate one \citep{gelman2012why,benjamini1995controlling}. The band itself is a
design-based sup-$t$ band on wave-pair CDF differences from the
stratified-PSU bootstrap \citep{rao1988resampling,wolter2007introduction},
an asymptotic guarantee to which the theory sections' finite-sample language
does not transfer.

\subsection{Cross-country transport and the safe adaptive band}
\label{sec:transport}

Theorem~\ref{thm:impossible} says the design bootstrap is the identifying
input; the transport method turns it into a band by choosing, target-blind,
among three constructions.

\paragraph{Three branches.} Take calibration curves $\{\Gtilde_c\}_{c=1}^K$
with per-country design SD $\widehat v_c(t)$ from the stratified-PSU
bootstrap. \emph{(i) The clustered conformal band} takes the conformal
quantile of the \emph{unstudentized} scores $\max_t|\Gtilde_c(t)|$, exact
for the survey-estimate target at any $K$ under Theorem~\ref{thm:exact}'s
symmetric centers. \emph{(ii) Deconvolution}
studentizes by the finite-$K$-guarded target scale
$\widehat s_{T}^2(t)=\max\!\big(s_{\text{plug}}^2(t)-[\,\overline{\widehat v^2}(t)-z\,\SE\,]_+,\ \text{floor}\big)$.
The guard subtracts a one-sided margin below the mean design variance
($z=1.645$ deployed), so that on the guard event the correction cannot
over-shrink. Under (A1) the branch's target scale, though not its realized
width, contracts by $\sqrt{1-\rho(t)^2}$ coordinate by coordinate.
\emph{(iii) The conservative band} inflates each score by the design SD,
$\max_t(|\Gtilde_c(t)|+z\,\widehat v_c(t))$, which dominates the clustered
score pointwise, so the conservative band \emph{contains} the clustered band
by construction.

\paragraph{Safe selector (Algorithm~\ref{alg:safe}).} A naive selector asks
\emph{can we deconvolve} and \emph{do we need to}; the safe selector adds
\emph{can we do it safely at this $K$?} Deconvolution is used only
when four target-blind gates pass, all computed from the calibration set.
The \emph{need} gate requires $\rhohat_{\text{LCB}}>\rho_0$, the aggregate
design share against a frozen operational cutoff. The \emph{reliability}
gate requires $D=\max_t\SE(\widehat s_T^2)/\widehat s_T^2\le\tau_D$, a
frozen bound on the finite-$K$ remainder (Proposition~\ref{prop:unreach}).
The \emph{efficiency} gate requires a certified width gain over the
conservative band, and the \emph{stability} gate requires the guarded scale
to stay above its floor. Otherwise the band is an anchor. All constants are
frozen before validation (Supplementary Material). Theorem~\ref{thm:safe}
shows that validity survives any selection between the anchors, the
deconvolution branch being charged its own budget only where its gate can
open at all ($K\ge94$). Three levels differ: the \emph{nominal}
$1-\alpha$; the \emph{theorem} level $1-\alpha-\varepsilon_{K,B}$ of
Theorem~\ref{thm:estimated}, whose remainder is unobservable; and the
\emph{validated operational} level reported when the procedure deconvolves,
$1-\alpha-\widehat\delta_{\text{UCB}}(D)$, a bound frozen before validation
and checked by it. The method ships as one call,
\texttt{dapcb(cal\_errors, v\_cal, center, alpha)}, returning band, branch,
and level.
