\section{Simulation and a design-preserving stress test}\label{sec:simulation}

Simulation establishes two facts: that attaching the band to the wrong unit collapses
coverage (Section~\ref{sec:wrongunit-sim}), and that the safe selector meets its
guarantees on data it has never seen. For the latter we develop the selector on one
simulation grid and then confirm the \emph{frozen} rule once on an independent design.
The separation is deliberate: the development grid may set the gate constants, but it
may not be reused as validation performance.

\subsection{The wrong unit collapses coverage}\label{sec:wrongunit-sim}

The most basic claim deserves a direct test: a band attached to the wrong unit covers
the object of interest far below nominal. On a ground-truth simulation ($K=30$
exchangeable countries, $T=6$ thresholds, error tensors correlated across rounds and
thresholds; Table~\ref{tab:wrongunit}), we ask each band for \emph{trajectory}
coverage---the whole country curve-path covered at once. All three bands are
unstudentized and finite-sample constructed, so the only thing that varies is the unit
of the score.

The per-round band, marginally valid at the round level, covers the whole trajectory only
$0.82$ at $L=2$ and $0.50$ at $L=8$; the marginal band collapses faster still. The
country-trajectory band holds $\approx0.90$ regardless of $L$, because the whole
trajectory is a single exchangeable unit and its sup-score obeys the finite-sample
order-statistic guarantee. This is the coverage face of the paper's thesis: the same
mechanism drives the certification-count collapse in Section~\ref{sec:political}, where
asking for the honest object---not asking it more strictly---removes the over-detection.
Pre-registered and reported as produced (\texttt{e28\_wrong\_unit\_coverage}).

\paragraph{Severity: what each rung can detect.} Coverage is only half of honesty;
the other half is saying what magnitude of decline each rung would detect. A
simulation calibrated to ESS-like design noise (threshold SE $v=0.012$; sup-$t$
certification at $\alpha=0.10$ over a four-threshold core; replication experiment
E32) gives the operating characteristics: for $80\%$ power the \emph{net} rung needs
a per-pair decline of $0.02$--$0.03$ CDF points over four pairs---realistic secular
erosion---while the \emph{persistent} rung needs $0.06$--$0.08$ per pair, roughly
the scale of the Greek crisis collapse, and its power \emph{falls} as the trajectory
lengthens (the conjunction hardens while the per-pair signal does not accumulate).
Two consequences discipline our substantive language. Non-certification at the
persistent rung is weak evidence of absence---it is a high-severity, low-power
object, informative when it \emph{passes}; and for secular-decline claims the
powered instrument is the net rung, which is why Section~\ref{sec:political}
co-headlines the net counts. Injecting known declines into the real microdata is
the corresponding real-data check, flagged for the archived replication.

The development grid that motivates and calibrates the safety gate, and a controlled
Gaussian illustration that isolates the finite-$K$ over-shrinkage mechanism behind the
$K{=}320$-only activation seen below, are given in the Supplementary Material.

\paragraph{Validation, in three honestly-labeled layers.} \emph{(i) The sealed
holdout} froze the selector and its constants (Appendix~\ref{app:spec}), sealed a
preregistration and seed manifest, and ran once on a grid disjoint from development
in $K$ ($\{25,40,80,160,320\}$), in $\rho$, and in data-generating process (ten
families). Its confirmatory status was subsequently compromised in two disclosed
ways: an audit found its scorer evaluated the S2 studentized band rather than the
band the package deploys, and its findings motivated the switch to the U0 base band
and the Theorem~\ref{thm:safe} architecture---so it functioned as development, and
we label it so. \emph{(ii) The corrected-scorer rerun} of the same $450$-cell grid
scores the deployed band verbatim (fresh seed; the original config file was not
preserved, which we disclose). \emph{(iii) A fresh seal}: because layers (i)--(ii)
had both touched the final design, we sealed one further validation of the final
pipeline---six new DGP families (lognormal country effects, AR(2) rounds, scale
mixtures, extreme design-effect dispersion, threshold-correlated noise, long
trajectories), new $K$ and $\rho$ grids, script hash recorded before first
execution, run once, reported as produced including a first-seal generator bug we
diagnosed and disclosed (replication experiment E33). Result: $120/120$ cells
floor-compatible, worst cell $0.878$, and zero deconvolution activations below
$K=94$, as the algorithmic floor requires. The numbers of all three layers are
reported exactly as produced (Table~\ref{tab:holdout}, Figure~\ref{fig:holdout}).

\emph{The design-aware layer behaves as designed.} In the sealed run all twelve cells
below the $0.88$ floor routed to base clustered PCB---the deconvolution branch never
once rode into a floor violation. Under the corrected scorer the deployed pipeline
passes every criterion outright: the selector reduces to PCB at low $\rho$ (width
ratio $1.005$), activates deconvolution only at $K{=}320$ (never for $K\le160$; $24$
cells, up to $99\%$ of draws at the highest $\rho$), and there delivers a band
$0.753\times$ the conservative width---wider than the sealed run's $0.577$ because
the $\alpha_{\text{dec}}$ budget of Theorem~\ref{thm:safe} prices the branch's
estimated-law remainder honestly---with worst $K{=}320$ cell coverage $0.900$.

\emph{The holdout exposed one base-method deficit, which we then resolved.} The
\emph{studentized} base band (in-sample pooled modulation) undercovered at small $K$
and low $\rho$ under the hardest DGPs, because dividing each calibration score by a
modulation estimated from the same clusters---which the unobserved target does not
share---breaks the exchangeability the conformal guarantee needs. We therefore deploy the
\emph{unstudentized} clustered band as the base branch: with no data-dependent
denominator the scores $R_c=\max_t|E_c(t)|$ are exchangeable, and the order-statistic
band is finite-sample exact at \emph{any} $K$,
$\Pr(\text{cover}) \ge \lceil(1-\alpha)(K{+}1)\rceil/(K{+}1) \ge 1-\alpha$, distribution-free
(Theorem~\ref{thm:exact}). A preregistered rerun on a fresh grid confirms the fix at a
width cost of $1.02$--$1.05\times$ on the real ESS transport bands; the granular
cell-by-cell results are in the Supplementary Material. The band the paper
deploys is thus finite-sample valid at the sample sizes comparative politics
actually has. A second, extreme-tail limitation of the Gaussian-calibrated remainder
bound $\widehat\delta_{\text{UCB}}$---confined to $0.02\%$ of draws and absorbed by the
conservative branch---is documented in the Supplementary Material. We do not claim uniform
nominal coverage; we claim---and the holdout confirms---that the design-aware correction
is never the \emph{cause} of undercoverage and is materially more efficient than
conservative where it safely activates.

\paragraph{Design-preserving stress test, honestly bounded.} A design-preserving
subsampling of the real AmericasBarometer shows the deployed selector never fires
even as PSUs are thinned toward the noise-dominated regime: pooled $\rhohat$ rises
to $\approx0.23$ and then \emph{turns over}. We flag the interpretation ourselves:
under the protocol's own $1/\sqrt f$ scaling the sweep was designed to cross
$\rho_0$ at the deepest subsampling, and the turnover coincides with the point where
$\approx1$ PSU remains per stratum---exactly where the $m$-of-$m$ bootstrap's
variance estimate collapses. The experiment therefore demonstrates a failure mode of
the $\rhohat$ \emph{diagnostic} under sparse-PSU designs (with the deployed band's
coverage remaining at or above nominal throughout), not a substantive saturation law;
the regime characterization of Section~\ref{sec:evidence} rests on the full-sample
$\rhohat$ values and the ESS subgroup scan, not on this sweep (Supplementary
Material).
