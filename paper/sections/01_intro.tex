\section{Introduction}

Comparative politics increasingly treats a national public as a
\emph{distribution} rather than a mean. Trust in parliament in a country and year
is a cumulative distribution function over a response scale. Repeated
cross-national surveys---the European Social Survey
\citep[ESS;][]{kaminska2017survey}, the World Values Survey (WVS), the
AmericasBarometer \citep{lapop2021pulse}---generate claims about how those
distributions evolve across waves. A second use follows: \emph{transporting}
the trajectories, predicting one country's from the others', for which
conformal methods are a natural tool, promising finite-sample,
distribution-free coverage \citep{vovk2005algorithmic,lei2018distribution}.

\paragraph{Two wrong units.} One principle organizes this paper: \emph{the
stochastic unit of a guarantee must coincide with the unit of the scientific
claim it certifies}. Claims from repeated surveys violate it twice. A
common inferential move builds the \emph{claim} --- ``democratic trust is
declining'' --- from a single wave-pair contrast and narrates it as a
country-level trend \citep{foa2016democratic,foa2017signs,valgardsson2025crisis}.
Our procedure instead covers the whole trajectory at once, with a partially
ordered family of claims read off one band, whose top --- persistence --- is
strictly stronger than the net trend the literature typically intends
(Section~\ref{sec:claims}). The second, subtler wrong unit is the
calibration: the objects conformal transport calibrates on are themselves
\emph{estimated} from complex probability samples, and existing constructions
plug them in as if exact \citep{dunn2023distribution,lee2023distribution}.

The two errors are distinct. Writing $F_{c, r}(t)$ for country $c$'s latent CDF at
threshold $t$ in wave $r$ and $\Ftilde_{c, r}(t)$ for its complex-survey estimate,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:decomp}
\Ftilde_{c, r}(t) - \mu_r(t)
\;=\; \underbrace{\big(F_{c, r}(t)-\mu_r(t)\big)}_{\text{transport error}}
\;+\; \underbrace{\big(\Ftilde_{c, r}(t)-F_{c, r}(t)\big)}_{\text{design error}},
\end{equation}
where $\mu_r$ is the transport center. A band calibrated on the observed
$\Ftilde$ scores treats the sum as the transport error alone: when the design
error is non-negligible the band is too wide, yet any correction subtracting the
survey noise can make it too narrow, being itself estimated from few countries.

\paragraph{Contributions.} We make four.
\emph{First, the claim-unit problem, and a procedure for it.} We separate the
claim unit (a wave-pair contrast standing in for a distribution-wide
trajectory) from the calibration unit (the omitted design-error layer
of~\eqref{eq:decomp}), and give a finite-sample simultaneous band over a
country's whole trajectory whose partially ordered claim family is read off one
coverage event. The two layers get two procedures: within-country
\emph{certification} is a design-based simultaneous sup-$t$ band over the
wave-pair contrast surface, while conformal transport calibrates cross-country
prediction --- the theory's finite-sample language attaches to the transport
band, not to the certification counts.
\emph{Second, an impossibility theorem for estimated calibration objects.}
Stated at the curve level of~\eqref{eq:decomp}: without information on the
design-noise law the latent transport-error law is non-identified, and no band
built on the observed curves can uniformly improve on the plug-in conformal
band while remaining valid (Theorem~\ref{thm:impossible}). Estimated
calibration is not a nuisance to correct away; it is an inferential boundary.
\emph{Third, the feasibility boundary of the correction, and a procedure that
respects it.} Even with the design-noise information supplied, the reliability
diagnostic obeys an algorithmic floor $D\ge\sqrt{2/(K-1)}$, so the deployed
correction requires $K\ge94$ exchangeable populations, and the two largest
surveys fail in opposite ways (Section~\ref{sec:evidence}). The
deployed selector is the theory's consequence: its anchor band is exact at any
$K$ under its symmetric centers and its leave-one-out deployment carries its
own finite-sample guarantee --- exactly valid after an explicit $K/(K{-}1)$
radius inflation, with an $O(1/K)$ remainder uninflated
(Theorem~\ref{thm:exact}; Supplementary Material) --- its anchor branches are
nested so selection among
them is validity-free, and the non-nested deconvolution branch is charged
a frozen budget only where its gate can open (Theorem~\ref{thm:safe}). One
unit down, on a region's departure from its national distribution
($K\approx230$--$290$), the same frozen selector does activate, and holding
out whole countries supports the region as the calibration unit
(Section~\ref{sec:evidence}). (The clustered band is machinery shared
with a companion paper on a non-survey domain, \citealp{park2026poverty}; new
here is what the survey layer forces --- the impossibility, the floor, the safe
selector, and the claim-family deployment.)
\emph{Fourth, two named reanalyses where the procedures change the substantive
picture} (Section~\ref{sec:political}). For European trust the family
certifies net decline in six of thirty (2018--2024), not the twenty flagged
marginally. Over the full 2002--2024 record no country certifies
persistence, twenty-three of thirty-three certify both declines and recoveries,
and eight certify span erosion, all read off one band at one level, with no
further correction within a country across wave pairs or endpoint choices. Globally,
applying a trajectory-persistence criterion to the Foa--Mounk
``deconsolidation'' battery on the WVS/EVS (1981--2022) cuts the wave-pair
certified set by $2.6$--$6.5\times$. Aggregating per-item
certifications then turns the correction into a discovery: the thirteen-country
\emph{certified core} concentrates in post-communist and Arab-Spring-aftermath
states.

Sections~\ref{sec:setup}--\ref{sec:discussion} proceed in that order: setup
and impossibility, the two procedures, theory, validation, the real-data
regime, the reanalyses, scope.
