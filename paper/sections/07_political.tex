\section{Political reanalysis: how many democracies really declined?}\label{sec:political}

We analyze trust in parliament (\texttt{trstprl}) and satisfaction with democracy
(\texttt{stfdem}) in ESS rounds 9--11 (fieldwork 2018--2024, the window whose
integrated files ship PSU/stratum identifiers), then extend every trajectory to
the full record (rounds 1--11, 2002--2024), asking at each rung of the hierarchy
in how many countries a decline survives.

The comparative literature has grown skeptical of a continent-wide trust decline
\citep{norris2017western,voeten2017people,wuttke2022europeans,valgardsson2025crisis};
our contribution is a formal instrument that says so, and the wrong-unit diagnosis
of \emph{why} the over-reading arises. The dominant global trend estimates
\citep{valgardsson2025crisis} rest on latent-trait models whose intervals carry no
design-based uncertainty layer \citep{taihusolt2024} and whose data end in 2019,
where our window begins. A mean can fall because a mode hollows out or
a tail thickens, with different substantive meanings \citep{dimaggio1996americans};
requiring the distribution's core to shift is the stronger claim.

\paragraph{A hierarchy of claims.} The claims differ in their unit. A
\emph{pairwise} claim asks whether some pair of waves shows a distributional
shift; \emph{any-pair} aggregates these within a country; \emph{net} asks whether
the first-to-last span declined; \emph{persistent country-wide} asks whether the
whole distribution declined \emph{simultaneously} across the trajectory---one band
holding over all wave pairs and thresholds at once; \emph{Bonferroni} further
controls the number of countries examined. Each rung is strictly stronger than the
last, and conflating them is exactly the over-detection this paper is about.

\paragraph{Which instrument certifies what.} Within-country \emph{certification}
is a design-based simultaneous (sup-$t$) band on wave-pair CDF differences from
the stratified-PSU bootstrap \citep{rao1988resampling,wolter2007introduction},
an asymptotic guarantee in which country exchangeability plays no role; the
conformal \emph{transport} band of Theorems~\ref{thm:exact}--\ref{thm:safe}
calibrates cross-country prediction and the regime diagnostics. Headline counts
are certification counts, and the theory sections' finite-sample language does
not transfer to them.

\paragraph{The counts collapse up the hierarchy.} For \texttt{trstprl} over rounds
9--11, a marginal pairwise reading flags $20$ of $30$ countries; an any-pair
design-aware band drops this to $12$; net first-to-last decline holds in $6$
(Austria, Belgium, Estonia, the United Kingdom, Greece, the Netherlands); and the
\emph{persistent, design-aware simultaneous} band over the preregistered low-trust
core---the strongest rung---certifies \textbf{one}, Greece, which also survives
Bonferroni across countries. One disclosure is essential: \emph{Greece fielded
only rounds 10 and 11 here}, so its trajectory is a single pair and the top rungs
coincide for it (as for Czechia and Israel, which do not certify). Among the $27$
two-pair countries \emph{none} certifies persistence, while the United Kingdom,
Belgium, and the Netherlands pass the \emph{plug-in} persistent rung and are
demoted by the design-aware layer alone. The honest co-headline: \textbf{net
decline in six of thirty; persistent decline in one, on a single pair}. The severity analysis
(Section~\ref{sec:simulation}) explains why: the persistent rung has $80\%$ power
only against crisis-scale declines, so its non-certifications are weak evidence of
absence. For \texttt{stfdem} the pattern repeats ($23$ marginal, $14$ any-pair,
$8$ net, one persistent---Greece, again one pair, failing Bonferroni there).
Greece is the case the literature regards as a crisis-era legitimacy collapse
\citep{armingeon2014democracy,torcal2014decline}, though those accounts concern
2009--2015 and the certified pair is 2020/21$\to$2023/24, a later episode.

\paragraph{The long window: the secular question, asked properly.} Extending every
trajectory to its full record (rounds 1--11; median six pairs, up to ten; rounds
1--8 weights-only) reframes the twenty-year narrative in both directions
(\texttt{e36}). \emph{Zero} of thirty-four countries---plug-in
included---certify persistent distribution-wide decline, and not merely for want
of power: reversing the inequality certifies \emph{recoveries}, and twenty-four
of the thirty-four certify at least one decline \emph{and} at least one recovery
($56$ rising pairs against $59$ falling; \texttt{e45}). Two decades of European
trust move in episodes---certified, not inferred from a null. Net erosion across
the whole span certifies in nine (Table~\ref{tab:magnitudes}), unchanged when the
weights-only rounds' variance is inflated by a design effect of $2.0$
(\texttt{e44}); Israel and Italy certify it with \emph{no individual certified
pair}---slow erosion invisible to any pairwise reading, the under-detection face
of the wrong unit. But a span claim is itself a two-wave contrast, and the
discipline this paper asks of others applies to its own strongest positive
claim: only three of the nine (Cyprus, Spain, the United Kingdom) survive
dropping either endpoint (\texttt{e43}). Ukraine's $28$-point decline runs from
the post-Orange-Revolution wave of 2005 to a wartime sample; Greece's and
Israel's spans likewise begin or end in a crisis wave. Those are certified
statements about the waves bracketing a record, not about the decades between.
The three endpoint-robust cases are what the long window supports.

\paragraph{The reclassified countries, and the honest word for them.} They are the
weak-signal, higher-noise cases: \emph{certified by the marginal plug-in but
inconclusive under the design-aware simultaneous band}---never ``false
positives,'' a term we reserve for simulation with known truth. Understated
design variance would make \emph{more} countries certify, so the counts err
inclusive: that protects the collapse-of-counts reading, not any individual
certification.

\paragraph{Two confounds this window cannot exclude.} Round-10 fieldwork
coincided with the pandemic's rally-round-the-flag trust movements, so
``persistent decline through this window'' is a claim made against a rally; and
nine countries switched to self-completion at round 10, confounding wave-pair
shifts with mode effects---a bias no design bootstrap absorbs. A mode audit built
from the data's own mode variable bounds this: the Greek pair is mode-constant,
net counts are unchanged when the switchers' confounded pairs are excluded (their
$9\to11$ endpoints are face-to-face), and only their any-pair claims are
affected.

\paragraph{Robustness across age groups.} A preregistered age-group analysis
confirms the result is not an artifact of pooling ages; youth certify zero
persistent declines as an underpowered null, not youth stability (Supplementary
Material).

\paragraph{A second, global target: Foa--Mounk deconsolidation.} We preregistered a
reanalysis of the ``democratic deconsolidation'' thesis
\citep{foa2016democratic,foa2017signs} on the WVS/EVS trends (1981--2022,
$59$--$77$ countries with $\ge2$ waves), recoding the five-item battery so that
deconsolidation is a persistent decline over each scale's preregistered core.
Globally the verdict is calibration; for the consolidated democracies the thesis
was formulated about it is denial. A \emph{marginal wave-pair reading of the same
data}---a criterion we construct to instantiate the bottom rung, since Foa and
Mounk's own empirics are cohort comparisons---flags $36$--$64$ countries per
item; the persistent, weights-aware object certifies $6$--$17$ (8--24\%), a
$2.6$--$6.5\times$ rung gap, with the design-aware layer demoting a further
$17$--$40\%$. Three caveats. The WVS ships no PSU/stratum identifiers, so the
weights-only bootstrap understates design variance, which can only
\emph{inflate} counts: the deff rerun confirms the gap is a lower bound---at
variance $\times1.5$ and $\times2.0$ the certified core holds at $13$ and $12$
countries and the gap widens to $3.0$--$8.3\times$. The per-item lists carry no
across-country familywise control, so we aggregate to multi-item
co-certification before interpreting geography. And the certification sets sit
below the $K\ge94$ bound, so every WVS band uses the full-$\alpha$ anchors of
Theorem~\ref{thm:safe}. The youth ``embrace authoritarianism'' claim is only
half-supported (Supplementary Material).

\paragraph{The certified core: where the surviving deconsolidation lives.}
Aggregating per-item certifications yields a positive characterization
(Figure~\ref{fig:core}; E30): of $38$ countries certified on $\ge1$ item, $13$ form
a \emph{certified core} on $\ge2$---Albania on four; Bosnia, Ecuador, Tunisia on
three; Azerbaijan, Finland, Ghana, Iraq, Lebanon, Rwanda, Switzerland, Trinidad and
Tobago, Uzbekistan on two. Three features bear on the debate.
\emph{First, the core is not the consolidated West}---it is post-communist and
Arab-Spring states, with fragile Latin American and African cases alongside, not
the mature democracies the thesis \citep{foa2016democratic,foa2017signs}
concerned. This formalizes with per-country error control what the
post-communist disillusionment literature described qualitatively: decline from
euphoric transition-era baselines
\citep{mishler1997trajectories,inglehart2003honeymoon}. Five core members
(Azerbaijan, Iraq, Lebanon, Rwanda, Uzbekistan) are electoral autocracies in the
V-Dem classification, where falling stated support reflects autocratic
legitimation, not ``deconsolidation'' in the consolidated-democracy sense. One objection deserves a direct answer: certified attitudes do not
forecast institutions. Core membership predicts subsequent V-Dem
electoral-democracy decline no better than the marginal flag ($n=13$ versus
$54$; Fisher $p=0.89$) --- a comparison far too small to establish decoupling,
which recent panel work anyway expects
\citep{ouattara2023distrusting,vandermeer2025pushing}. The estimand here is the
attitude trajectory itself; certification is descriptive error control, not a
forecast, and the marginal reading buys no predictive advantage for its extra
claims. The West enters the
\emph{core} exactly twice (Finland, Switzerland, on the strongman/army pair), with
single-item entries elsewhere (Canada, Sweden, Norway, the United Kingdom). No
consolidated Western democracy certifies on more than one item, and none on
support for a democratic system---consistent with \citet{wuttke2022europeans}.
\emph{Second, the Finnish--Swiss pair is flagged, not headlined.} Persistent rising
openness to strongman/army rule in two of the most stable democracies contradicts
most published accounts; these items carry documented translation and
response-option instabilities, and both countries' trend files interleave EVS and
WVS administrations---exactly the distribution-wide shift an administration change
produces, while our machinery corrects sampling error only. We report the pair as a
candidate finding conditional on a within-program measurement audit.
\emph{Third, the celebrated backsliding cases are narrower than their
reputation.} Turkey, the Philippines, and Mexico certify only on importance of
democracy, an item entering the battery in the mid-2000s, so ``only on
imp\_dem'' mixes substance with differential window length and should be held
loosely. Wave coverage varies by country and item, making multi-item
certification harder where fewer are measured; the counts are descriptive
aggregation and the regional labels are groupings, not covariates.
