\section{Political reanalysis: how many democracies really declined?}\label{sec:political}

We analyze trust in parliament (\texttt{trstprl}) and satisfaction with democracy
(\texttt{stfdem}) in ESS rounds 9--11 (fieldwork 2018--2024, the window whose
integrated files ship PSU/stratum identifiers), then extend every trajectory to
the full record (rounds 1--11, 2002--2024), asking at each rung of the hierarchy
in how many countries a decline survives.

The comparative literature has grown skeptical of a continent-wide trust decline
\citep{norris2017western,voeten2017people,wuttke2022europeans,valgardsson2025crisis};
our contribution is a formal instrument that says so, and the wrong-unit diagnosis
of \emph{why} the over-reading arises. The dominant global trend estimates
\citep{valgardsson2025crisis} rest on latent-trait models whose intervals carry no
design-based uncertainty layer \citep{taihusolt2024} and whose data end in 2019,
where our window begins. A mean can fall because a mode hollows out or
a tail thickens, with different substantive meanings \citep{dimaggio1996americans};
requiring the distribution's core to shift is the stronger claim.

\paragraph{A hierarchy of claims.} The claims differ in their unit. A
\emph{pairwise} claim asks whether some pair of waves shows a distributional
shift; \emph{any-pair} aggregates these within a country; \emph{net} asks whether
the first-to-last span declined; \emph{persistent country-wide} asks whether the
whole distribution declined \emph{simultaneously} across the trajectory---one band
holding over all wave pairs and thresholds at once; \emph{Bonferroni} further
controls the number of countries examined. Each rung is strictly stronger than the
last, and conflating them is exactly the over-detection this paper is about.

\paragraph{Which instrument certifies what.} Within-country \emph{certification} is
a design-based simultaneous (sup-$t$) band on wave-pair CDF differences from the
stratified-PSU bootstrap \citep{rao1988resampling,wolter2007introduction}---an
asymptotic design-consistency guarantee in which country exchangeability plays no
role. The conformal \emph{transport} band of
Theorems~\ref{thm:exact}--\ref{thm:safe} calibrates cross-country prediction and
the regime diagnostics. Headline counts are certification counts; the theory
sections' finite-sample language does not transfer to them.

\paragraph{The counts collapse up the hierarchy.} For \texttt{trstprl} over rounds
9--11, a marginal pairwise reading flags $20$ of $30$ countries; an any-pair
design-aware band drops this to $12$; net first-to-last decline holds in $6$
(Austria, Belgium, Estonia, the United Kingdom, Greece, the Netherlands); and the
\emph{persistent, design-aware simultaneous} band over the preregistered low-trust
core---the strongest rung---certifies \textbf{one}, Greece, which also survives
Bonferroni across countries. One disclosure is essential: \emph{Greece fielded
only rounds 10 and 11 here}, so its trajectory is a single pair and the top rungs
coincide for it (as for Czechia and Israel, which do not certify). Among the $27$
two-pair countries \emph{none} certifies persistence, while the United Kingdom,
Belgium, and the Netherlands pass the \emph{plug-in} persistent rung and are
demoted by the design-aware layer alone. The honest co-headline: \textbf{net
decline in six of thirty; persistent decline in one, on a single pair}. The severity analysis
(Section~\ref{sec:simulation}) explains why: the persistent rung has $80\%$ power
only against crisis-scale declines, so its non-certifications are weak evidence of
absence. For \texttt{stfdem} the pattern repeats ($23$ marginal, $14$ any-pair,
$8$ net, one persistent---Greece, again one pair, failing Bonferroni there).
Greece is the case the literature regards as a crisis-era legitimacy collapse
\citep{armingeon2014democracy,torcal2014decline}, though those accounts concern
2009--2015 and the certified pair is 2020/21$\to$2023/24, a later episode.

\paragraph{The long window: the secular question, asked properly.} Extending every
trajectory to its full record (rounds 1--11; median six pairs, up to ten; rounds
1--8 weights-only, which errs \emph{inclusive}) reframes the twenty-year narrative
in both directions (\texttt{e36}; Supplementary Material). \emph{Zero} of
thirty-four countries---plug-in included---certify persistent distribution-wide
decline over their full trajectory: two decades of European trust move as episodes
with recoveries, not a monotone slide, as the severity analysis predicts. Net
erosion over the span is certified in nine (Cyprus, Spain, France, the United
Kingdom, Greece, Hungary, Israel, Italy, Ukraine)---Greece now over its full
2002--2024 span, resolving the single-pair caveat---and Israel and Italy certify
with \emph{no individual certified pair}: slow erosion invisible to any pairwise
reading. The wrong unit over-detects in one direction and under-detects in the
other.

\paragraph{The reclassified countries, and the honest word for them.} They are the
weak-signal, higher-noise cases (scatter in the Supplementary Material):
\emph{certified by the marginal plug-in but inconclusive under the design-aware
simultaneous band}---never ``false positives,'' a term we reserve for simulation
with known truth. Because understated design variance would make \emph{more}
countries certify (the $m$-of-$m$ bias, Section~\ref{sec:discussion}), the counts
err inclusive, protecting the collapse-of-counts reading though not any individual
certification.

\paragraph{Two confounds this window cannot exclude.} Round-10 fieldwork coincided
with the pandemic's documented rally-round-the-flag trust movements, so
``persistent decline through this window'' is a claim made against a rally; and
several countries switched to self-completion modes at round 10, confounding
wave-pair CDF shifts with mode effects---a bias no design bootstrap absorbs
(Section~\ref{sec:discussion}). The mode audit bounds this: the Greek pair is
mode-constant, net counts are unchanged excluding the nine self-completion
switchers' confounded pairs (their $9\to11$ endpoints are face-to-face), and only
those countries' any-pair claims are affected (Supplementary Material). Both confounds cut against strong
readings in either direction---one more reason the net rung carries this
window's substantive weight.

\paragraph{Robustness across age groups.} A preregistered age-group analysis
confirms ``only Greece'' is not an artifact of pooling ages (for trust in
parliament it holds within both the 50+ and 30--49 groups; for satisfaction with
democracy within 50+); youth certify zero persistent declines as an underpowered
null, not youth stability (Supplementary Material).

\paragraph{A second, global target: Foa--Mounk deconsolidation.} We preregistered a
reanalysis of the ``democratic deconsolidation'' thesis
\citep{foa2016democratic,foa2017signs} on the WVS/EVS trends (1981--2022,
$59$--$77$ countries with $\ge2$ waves), recoding the five-item battery so that
deconsolidation is a persistent decline over each scale's preregistered core. The
verdict is calibration, not denial. A \emph{marginal wave-pair reading of the same
data}---a criterion we construct to instantiate the bottom rung; Foa and Mounk's
own empirics are cohort comparisons---flags $36$--$64$ countries per item; the
persistent, weights-aware object certifies $6$--$17$ (8--24\%), a
$2.6$--$6.5\times$ rung gap, with the design-aware layer demoting a further
$17$--$40\%$ per item. Three caveats govern these counts. The
WVS ships no PSU/stratum identifiers, so the weights-only bootstrap understates
design variance; understated variance can only \emph{inflate} certified counts,
and the deff-sensitivity rerun confirms the gap is a lower bound: at bootstrap
variance $\times1.5$ and $\times2.0$ the certified core holds at $13$ and $12$
countries while the gap widens to $3.0$--$8.3\times$ (Supplementary Material). The per-item lists
carry no across-country familywise control---a handful of boundary-null
certifications per item is expected---so we aggregate to multi-item
co-certification before interpreting geography. And the certification sets sit
below the $K\ge94$ bound, so every WVS band uses the full-$\alpha$ anchors of
Theorem~\ref{thm:safe} (Section~\ref{sec:evidence}'s gate probe describes the
full item-coverage sets, a different denominator). The
youth ``embrace authoritarianism'' claim is only half-supported (Supplementary
Material).

\paragraph{The certified core: where the surviving deconsolidation lives.}
Aggregating per-item certifications yields a positive characterization
(Figure~\ref{fig:core}; E30): of $38$ countries certified on $\ge1$ item, $13$ form
a \emph{certified core} on $\ge2$---Albania on four; Bosnia, Ecuador, Tunisia on
three; Azerbaijan, Finland, Ghana, Iraq, Lebanon, Rwanda, Switzerland, Trinidad and
Tobago, Uzbekistan on two. Three features bear on the debate.
\emph{First, the core is not the consolidated West.} It is dominated by
post-communist states and the Arab-Spring aftermath, with fragile Latin American and
African cases alongside---not the mature democracies the thesis
\citep{foa2016democratic,foa2017signs} was formulated about. This partly formalizes,
with per-country error control, what the post-communist disillusionment literature
described qualitatively---decline from euphoric transition-era baselines
\citep{mishler1997trajectories,inglehart2003honeymoon}---and five core members
(Azerbaijan, Iraq, Lebanon, Rwanda, Uzbekistan) are electoral autocracies in the
V-Dem 2019 classification (Supplementary Material), where falling stated support
reflects autocratic legitimation and possible preference falsification, not
``deconsolidation'' in the consolidated-democracy sense. A second V-Dem cross-check
bounds the predictive reading: core membership does not anticipate near-term
institutional backsliding better than the marginal flag---attitudes and
institutions move separately here, as the recent panel literature would predict
\citep{ouattara2023distrusting,vandermeer2025pushing}. The West enters the
\emph{core} exactly twice (Finland, Switzerland, on the strongman/army pair), with
single-item entries elsewhere: Canada and Sweden on importance of democracy, Norway
and the United Kingdom on parliament confidence. The accurate summary: no
consolidated Western democracy certifies on more than one item, and none on support
for a democratic system---consistent with \citet{wuttke2022europeans}---not that
the West is absent.
\emph{Second, the Finnish--Swiss pair is flagged, not headlined.} Persistent rising
openness to strongman/army rule in two of the most stable democracies contradicts
most published accounts; these items carry documented translation and
response-option instabilities, and both countries' trend files interleave EVS and
WVS administrations---exactly the distribution-wide shift an administration change
produces, while our machinery corrects sampling error only. We report the pair as a
candidate finding conditional on a within-program measurement audit.
\emph{Third, the celebrated backsliding cases are narrower than their
reputation---with a window caveat.} Turkey, the Philippines, and Mexico certify
only on importance of democracy, an item entering the battery only from the
mid-2000s; ``only on imp\_dem'' therefore mixes substance with differential window
length, and the reading---persistent downgrading of democracy's importance without
wholesale attitudinal collapse---should be held loosely. Wave coverage varies by
country and item (per-country table flagged for the archived replication), so
multi-item certification is harder where fewer are measured. The counts are
descriptive aggregation; the regional labels are groupings, not covariates.
