\section{Political reanalysis: how many democracies really declined?}\label{sec:political}

The method's payoff is a sharp distinction between what marginal analysis appears to
show and what a design-aware, simultaneous band certifies. We analyze trust in
parliament (\texttt{trstprl}) and satisfaction with democracy (\texttt{stfdem}) in
ESS rounds 9--11 (fieldwork 2018--2024; the window in which PSU and stratum
identifiers ship in the integrated file---sample-design files for earlier rounds
exist separately and would extend the trajectory, a revision we flag rather than
claim), treating each country's attitude distribution as the object and asking, at
each rung of a guarantee hierarchy, in how many countries a decline survives.

The comparative literature has itself grown skeptical of a continent-wide trust decline:
where \citet{foa2016democratic} saw ``democratic deconsolidation,''
\citet{norris2017western} saw trendless fluctuation, \citet{voeten2017people} saw
specification-fragile artifacts, and \citet{wuttke2022europeans}---analyzing eighteen
democracies under preregistration---found endorsement of self-government largely
intact; the most comprehensive recent accounting finds no universal secular decline
in institutional trust at all \citep{valgardsson2025crisis}. This qualitative consensus
holds that ``trust is falling everywhere'' is an over-reading; our contribution is a
formal instrument that says so, and the wrong-unit diagnosis of \emph{why} it arises. A
mean can fall because a mode hollows out or a tail thickens, with different substantive
meanings \citep{dimaggio1996americans}; requiring the whole distribution to shift is the
stronger, and more honest, claim.

\paragraph{A hierarchy of claims.} The claims differ in their unit and are not
interchangeable. A \emph{pairwise} claim asks whether some pair of waves shows a
distributional shift; an \emph{any-pair} claim aggregates these within a country; a
\emph{net} claim asks whether the first-to-last span declined; a \emph{persistent
country-wide} claim asks whether the \emph{whole} distribution declined
\emph{simultaneously} across the trajectory---a single band that must hold over all
wave pairs and all thresholds at once; and a \emph{Bonferroni-across-countries} claim
further controls the number of countries examined. Each rung is strictly stronger
than the last, and conflating them is exactly the over-detection this paper is about.

\paragraph{Which instrument certifies what.} Two distinct instruments appear in this
section, and they carry different guarantees. The \emph{certification} of a
within-country decline is a design-based simultaneous (sup-$t$) band on wave-pair
CDF differences, built from the stratified-PSU bootstrap
\citep{rao1988resampling,wolter2007introduction}---a design-consistency
(asymptotic) guarantee in which the country effect cancels and country
exchangeability plays no role. The \emph{transport} band of
Theorems~\ref{thm:exact}--\ref{thm:safe}---the finite-sample-exact conformal
object---calibrates cross-country prediction and drives the simulation results and
the regime diagnostics of Section~\ref{sec:evidence}. Headline counts below are
certification counts; we say so once here so that the finite-sample language of the
theory sections is not silently transferred to them.

\paragraph{The counts collapse up the hierarchy.} For \texttt{trstprl} over ESS
rounds 9--11 (fieldwork 2018--2024), a marginal pairwise reading flags $20$ of $30$
countries; requiring an any-pair design-aware band drops this to $12$; the net
first-to-last decline holds in $6$ (Austria, Belgium, Estonia, the United Kingdom,
Greece, the Netherlands); and the \emph{persistent, design-aware simultaneous} band
over the preregistered low-trust core of the scale---the strongest rung---is
certified in \textbf{one} country, Greece, which also survives the Bonferroni
correction across countries. One disclosure is essential to reading that count:
\emph{Greece fielded only rounds 10 and 11 in this window}, so its trajectory is a
single wave pair and the persistent, net, and any-pair rungs coincide for it---the
top rung degenerates, for the sole surviving country, to the strongest available
version of a one-pair claim (the same holds for Czechia and Israel, which do not
certify). Among the $27$ countries with two pairs, \emph{none} certifies
persistence, while the United Kingdom, Belgium, and the Netherlands pass the
\emph{plug-in} persistent rung and are demoted by the design-aware layer alone. The
honest co-headline is therefore: \textbf{net decline in six of thirty; persistent
decline in one, and that one on a single pair}. The severity analysis of
Section~\ref{sec:simulation} explains why the net rung must carry the
secular-decline question: at ESS-like noise the persistent rung has $80\%$ power
only against declines of $0.06$--$0.08$ CDF points per pair---crisis-scale
collapses---so its non-certifications are weak evidence of absence, while a
certification that does pass it is correspondingly strong. For \texttt{stfdem} the same pattern
holds ($23$ marginal, $14$ any-pair, $8$ net, one persistent, again Greece---again on
one pair), though there the Greek decline does \emph{not} survive the additional
Bonferroni rung; the across-countries correction is passed for trust in parliament
only. Greece is the case the substantive literature independently regards as a
crisis-era collapse of legitimacy \citep{armingeon2014democracy,torcal2014decline},
though we note those accounts concern 2009--2015 and the certified pair here is
2020/21$\to$2023/24, a later episode.
The headline is that a \emph{persistent distribution-wide} decline---the strong claim
casual readings imply---is certifiable in a single country over this window
(Figure~\ref{fig:hierarchy}).

\paragraph{Which countries are reclassified, and the honest word for it.} The
reclassified countries (Figure~\ref{fig:reclass}) are the weak-signal, higher-noise
cases, where the apparent trend lies within the design uncertainty the survey warrants.
We describe them as \emph{certified by the marginal plug-in but inconclusive under the
design-aware simultaneous band}---never as ``false positives,'' a term we reserve for
the simulation where ground truth is known. Because understated design variance would
make \emph{more} countries certify, the certified counts err on the inclusive side
(the PSU bootstrap resamples $m$-of-$m$ without rescaling, a known downward variance
bias; Section~\ref{sec:discussion}), which protects the collapse-of-counts reading
though not any individual certification.

\paragraph{Two confounds this window cannot exclude.} First, round-10 fieldwork
coincided with the pandemic: institutional trust exhibited documented
rally-round-the-flag movements in 2020--21, so ``persistent decline through this
window'' is a claim made against a rally, and non-certification of two-pair countries
partly reflects that. Second, several countries switched to self-completion modes at
round 10, and wave-pair CDF shifts across a mode switch confound mode effects with
attitude change---a \emph{bias} problem that no design bootstrap absorbs and that our
machinery, which corrects sampling variance only, does not address
(Section~\ref{sec:discussion} states this scope condition). A mode/fieldwork audit
of the certified pairs, and reruns excluding mode-switching pairs, require the
microdata and are flagged for the archived replication rerun. Both confounds cut
against strong readings in either direction, which is one more reason the net rung,
not the persistent rung, should carry the substantive weight of this window.

\paragraph{Robustness across age groups.} A preregistered age-group analysis
(Appendix~\ref{app:spec}) confirms the ``only Greece'' result is not an artifact of
pooling ages---it holds within the older (50+) and middle (30--49) groups---while youth
(18--29) certify zero persistent declines as an underpowered null rather than youth
stability, and emphatically not a story of youth-led democratic disaffection. The full
age-band results are in the Supplementary Material.

\paragraph{A second, global target: Foa--Mounk deconsolidation.} To show the correction
is not a European artifact, we preregistered a reanalysis of the Foa--Mounk
``democratic deconsolidation'' thesis \citep{foa2016democratic,foa2017signs} on the
World Values Survey / EVS trends (1981--2022, 59--77 countries with $\ge2$ waves;
per the replication package's preregistration), recoding the deconsolidation battery
(importance of democracy, rejection of strong-leader and army rule, support for a
democratic system, confidence in parliament) so that deconsolidation is a persistent
decline over the preregistered core of each response scale. The verdict is
calibration, not denial. A \emph{marginal wave-pair reading of the same data}---a
criterion we construct to instantiate the bottom rung, not one any published author
uses; Foa and Mounk's own empirics are cohort-level comparisons---flags $36$--$64$
countries per item; the persistent, weights-aware object is certified in $6$--$17$
(8--24\% of countries), a $2.6$--$6.5\times$ gap between the rungs. The design-aware
layer demotes a further $17$--$40\%$ per item ($\approx30\%$ on average) at the
persistent bar. Three caveats govern these counts. The WVS ships no PSU or stratum
identifiers, so the correction is a \emph{weights-only} respondent bootstrap that
understates design variance under the clustered designs most WVS surveys use;
understated variance can only \emph{inflate} certified counts, so the rung gap is, if
anything, a lower bound---a design-effect sensitivity rerun (variance
$\times1.5$--$2$) is flagged for the archived replication. The per-item country lists
carry no across-country familywise control (unlike the ESS hierarchy's Bonferroni
rung); at $\alpha=0.10$ over $59$--$77$ screened countries a handful of boundary-null
certifications per item is expected, which is why we aggregate to multi-item
co-certification before interpreting geography. And the certification sets
($K=59$--$77$ per item, the $\ge2$-wave subsets) sit below the $K\ge94$ feasibility
bound, so every WVS band rides the full-$\alpha$ anchors of
Theorem~\ref{thm:safe}; the $K\approx95$--$105$ figures in
Section~\ref{sec:evidence}'s gate probe describe the full item-coverage sets, a
different denominator. The youth ``embrace authoritarianism'' claim is only
half-supported (Supplementary Material). The point is the cross-family robustness of
the wrong-unit correction, from the dramatic ($20\to1$, the one on a single pair,
for European trust) to the moderate ($2.6$--$6.5\times$ globally).

\paragraph{The certified core: where the surviving deconsolidation lives.} The
instrument does not only subtract; aggregating the per-item certifications yields a
positive characterization of where persistent deconsolidation is concentrated
(Figure~\ref{fig:core}; replication experiment E30). Of the $38$ countries certified
on at least one battery item, $13$ form a \emph{certified core} certified on two or
more of the five: Albania on four; Bosnia and Herzegovina, Ecuador, and Tunisia on
three; and Azerbaijan, Finland, Ghana, Iraq, Lebanon, Rwanda, Switzerland, Trinidad
and Tobago, and Uzbekistan on two. Three features of this set bear on the debate,
each with a stated caveat.
\emph{First, the core is not the consolidated West.} It is dominated by
post-communist states of the Balkans, Caucasus, and Central Asia and by the
Arab-Spring aftermath (Tunisia, Lebanon, Iraq), with fragile Latin American and
African cases alongside---young or partial democracies and post-authoritarian
societies, not the mature democracies the deconsolidation thesis
\citep{foa2016democratic,foa2017signs} was formulated about. This geography partly
echoes what the post-communist disillusionment literature anticipated---decline from
euphoric transition-era baselines \citep{mishler1997trajectories,
inglehart2003honeymoon}---so the certification is best read as formalizing, with
per-country error control, a concentration that literature described qualitatively;
and several core members (Rwanda, Uzbekistan, Azerbaijan) are electoral autocracies,
where falling stated support is a phenomenon of autocratic legitimation and possible
preference falsification, not ``deconsolidation'' in the consolidated-democracy
sense. Consolidated Western democracies enter the \emph{core} exactly twice---Finland
and Switzerland, on the strongman/army-rule pair---though the single-item set adds
Western entries on other items: Canada and Sweden certify on importance of democracy,
and Norway and the United Kingdom on confidence in parliament. The accurate summary
is that no consolidated Western democracy certifies on more than one item, and none
certifies on support for a democratic system---consistent with the preregistered
finding that endorsement of self-government remains intact
\citep{wuttke2022europeans}---not that the West is absent.
\emph{Second, the Finnish--Swiss pair is flagged, not headlined.} Persistent rising
openness to strongman and army rule in two of the most stable democracies contradicts
most published accounts, and precisely these battery items carry documented
translation and response-option instabilities across waves, and both countries'
trend files interleave EVS and WVS administrations; a distribution-wide shift is
exactly what an administration or wording change produces, and our machinery corrects
sampling error only (Section~\ref{sec:discussion}). We therefore report the pair as
a candidate finding conditional on a within-program measurement audit, which
requires the microdata.
\emph{Third, the celebrated backsliding cases are narrower than their reputation---with
a window caveat.} Turkey, the Philippines, and Mexico certify only on the
importance-of-democracy item. That item enters the battery only from the mid-2000s,
so its persistence window is shorter than the other items' (which reach back to
1981/1995); ``only on imp\_dem'' therefore mixes substance with differential window
length, and the interpretive reading---persistent public downgrading of democracy's
importance without wholesale attitudinal collapse---should be held with
corresponding looseness. More generally, wave coverage varies by country and item,
so multi-item certification is harder where fewer items and waves are measured; a
per-country table of qualifying waves and programs is flagged for the archived
replication. The counts are descriptive aggregation of per-item certifications; the
regional labels are descriptive groupings, not covariates.
