\section{Theory}\label{sec:theory}

We state validity for the oracle, the estimated-law procedure, and the deployed
selector, then efficiency. Proofs are in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}; each theorem has
a machine-checked contract test.

\begin{theorem}[Oracle validity]\label{thm:oracle}
Suppose the countries are exchangeable and the design law is known.
\emph{(a)} The clustered band is finite-sample valid for the survey-estimate target:
$\Pr(\Ftilde_{\text{new}}\in\widehat B)\ge1-\alpha$, using exchangeability only.
\emph{(b)} For the latent target the same band satisfies, for every $u>0$,
$\Pr(F_{\text{new}}\in\widehat B)\ge1-\alpha-\Pr(\xi<-u)-
\Pr(\widetilde R\in(\widehat q-u,\widehat q\,])$: a noise-tail term plus a local
anti-concentration term, vanishing with the design-noise scale ($\rho^{2/3}$ under
variance-only tails; $\rho$ up to logarithms under sub-Gaussian noise;
Appendix~\ref{app:proofs} states the conditions, including where independence is
used). Symmetric design noise alone does \emph{not} make the band conservative for
the latent target---a bimodal latent law furnishes a counterexample (Supplementary
Material)---which is why we report the observed $\rho$ rather than assuming
conservativeness.
\emph{(c)} The deconvolution band with the true target scale is valid for the latent
target---finite-sample \emph{exact} under the scale-family assumption (A1) of
Theorem~\ref{thm:estimated}---with width a factor $\sqrt{1-\rho^2}+o(1)$ of the
clustered band.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Exact finite-$K$ validity of the deployed base band]\label{thm:exact}
Let the base band use the \emph{unstudentized} cluster score $R_c=\max_t|E_c(t)|$ (no
data-dependent modulation), with a center satisfying the symmetric-construction
condition: fixed, split-fold, per-unit from that unit's own data (the deployed
own-previous-round centers), or a symmetric function of all $K{+}1$ curves
(Appendix~\ref{app:proofs} states the four cases). If the countries are exchangeable,
then for \emph{every} $K$ the band $\widehat B=\text{center}\pm\widehat q$, with
$\widehat q$ the $\lceil(1-\alpha)(K{+}1)\rceil$-th order statistic of $\{R_c\}$,
satisfies
$\Pr(F_{\text{new}}\in\widehat B)\ge\lceil(1-\alpha)(K{+}1)\rceil/(K{+}1)\ge1-\alpha$,
distribution-free. Two asymmetries are excluded by construction: in-sample
modulation (an $O(g/K)$ self-inclusion deficit) and grand-mean centers containing
their own unit (an anti-conservative $O(1/K)$ bias, removed by leave-one-out
centers); Appendix~\ref{app:proofs} quantifies both. On the near-homoskedastic real
curves the width cost of dropping the modulation is $\le5\%$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Estimated-law validity]\label{thm:estimated}
Assume the observed and latent score curves sit in a common scale family indexed by
the design variance (A1), the sup-score law satisfies an anti-concentration bound
near its upper quantile (A2), and fourth moments and an unbiased size-$B$ design
bootstrap exist (A3) --- Appendix~\ref{app:proofs} states all three precisely.
Then \emph{(i)} with oracle scales the deconvolution band is finite-sample
\emph{exact} for the latent target at every $K$, and \emph{(ii)} with estimated
scales it satisfies
$\Pr(F_{\text{new}}\in\widehat B)\ge 1-\alpha-\varepsilon_{K,B}$ with
$\varepsilon_{K,B}=O\big(\sqrt{\log(KT)/\min(K,B)}\,\big)$, proved by a sandwich and
concentration argument with explicit constants. The finite-$K$-safe scale
$\widehat s_T^2$ (Section~\ref{sec:method}) subtracts a lower confidence bound on the
design variance, making the scale-error term one-sided (wide, not narrow) outside an
$\alpha_2$-probability event. Some shape restriction is not removable---the bimodal
counterexample of Theorem~\ref{thm:oracle}(b) defeats any latent-target band without
one---and (A1) is one sufficient choice, strong enough to make the distributional
matching exact; alternatives (e.g.\ smoothness conditions under a known noise law,
as in the deconvolution literature) trade finite-sample exactness for weaker
assumptions.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Safe-adaptive finite-$K$ validity---the deployed pipeline]
\label{thm:safe}
Deploy Algorithm~\ref{alg:safe} with unstudentized PCB and conservative branches
(scores $\max_t|E_c(t)|$ and $\max_t(|E_c(t)|+z\widehat v_c(t))$, both at level
$\alpha_{\text{anchor}}$) and the deconvolution branch at its own budget
$\alpha_{\text{dec}}$, where $(\alpha_{\text{anchor}},\alpha_{\text{dec}})=(\alpha,0)$
when the reliability gate is infeasible at the observed $K$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:unreach}: $K<94$, which covers every certification sample
analyzed in this paper, including the WVS $\ge2$-wave sets at $K=59$--$77$) and
otherwise $\alpha_{\text{dec}}=\min\{\max(0.1\alpha,\,3/(K{+}1)),\,\alpha/2\}$ with
$\alpha_{\text{anchor}}=\alpha-\alpha_{\text{dec}}$, a frozen function of $K$ alone.
Then, finite-sample and with \emph{no conditions on the selection rule},
\[
\Pr\big(F_{\text{new},r}(t)\in\widehat B(r,t)\ \forall r,t\big)\;\ge\;
1-\alpha-\varepsilon_{K,B}\,\mathbf 1\{K\ge94\},
\]
where $\varepsilon_{K,B}$ is Theorem~\ref{thm:estimated}'s estimated-law
remainder, attached to the deconvolution branch only---the $K\ge94$ clause therefore
inherits that theorem's assumptions (A1)--(A3), while the $K<94$ clause needs
exchangeability only---and reported through the observable diagnostic
$\widehat\delta_{\text{UCB}}(D)$ when that branch fires.
\end{theorem}

The proof (Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}) uses no conditional-on-selection step: a
nested-band lemma (the conservative score dominates the PCB score pointwise, and any
selection between nested bands anchored at an exact band is validity-free---an
instance of nested-conformal machinery \citep{gupta2022nested,yang2024selection};
our contribution is engineering the branches so it applies) plus a union bound
charging the non-nested deconvolution branch its own budget, only where its gate can
open. The gates therefore carry no validity burden; at survey scale the deployed
guarantee is exact $1-\alpha$, full stop.

\paragraph{Efficiency.} With epistemic status labeled: \emph{(AW-1, proved)} the
low-$\rho$ deconvolution/PCB width ratio is $1-\tfrac12\rho^2+O(\rho^4)$;
\emph{(AW-2)} conservative $\supseteq$ PCB holds pointwise by construction, while
its domination by the Bonferroni envelope rests on an unproved first-order
expansion; \emph{(AW-3, proved)} selection cannot inflate miscoverage
(Theorem~\ref{thm:safe}). The oracle inequality AW-4 (open) and the empirically
fitted $O(g/K)$ modulation deficit are in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.
