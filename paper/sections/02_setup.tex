\section{Setup and the two targets}\label{sec:setup}

The object we deploy is the exact simultaneous band of Theorem~\ref{thm:exact}; the
impossibility below (Theorem~\ref{thm:impossible}) is a scope boundary delimiting when a
design-aware correction to that band is even available.

\paragraph{Objects.} We observe $K$ source countries, indexed $c=1,\dots,K$, treated
as exchangeable units (the ``population'' or cluster is the exchangeable object, not
the individual respondent). Each country has a latent attitude trajectory
$F_{c,r}(t)$, a CDF over a fixed grid of thresholds $t\in\{t_1,\dots,t_T\}$ and survey
rounds $r$. We do not see $F_{c,r}$; we see a complex-survey estimate
$\Ftilde_{c,r}(t)=F_{c,r}(t)+S_{c,r}(t)$, where $S_{c,r}$ is design (sampling) error
with a design variance $v_{c,r}(t)^2$ that a stratified-PSU / replicate-weight
bootstrap can estimate. Define the transport score of a country as its worst-case
standardized deviation from the transport center,
\[
R_c \;=\; \max_{r,t}\frac{|F_{c,r}(t)-\mu_r(t)|}{\sigma(t)},
\qquad
\widetilde{R}_c \;=\; R_c + \xi_c,
\]
where $\widetilde{R}_c$ is the same quantity computed from the observed $\Ftilde$ and
$\xi_c$ is the induced design contamination. (Because $R$ is a supremum, the induced
$\xi_c$ is not exactly mean-zero or independent of $R_c$---taking a max over noisy
coordinates biases the score upward; the additive, centered score model here is a
working approximation at the score scale, made exact at the curve scale by the
scale-family assumption of Theorem~\ref{thm:estimated}, and the direction of its
failure---upward-biased observed scores---is conservative for the plug-in band.) The scalar that governs the whole
problem is the design-to-\emph{total} standard-deviation ratio
\[
\rho \;=\; \frac{\text{SD}(\xi)}{\text{SD}(\widetilde R)}
\;=\;\sqrt{\frac{\overline{v^2}}{s_R^2+\overline{v^2}}}\;\in\;[0,1),
\]
with $\rho\to0$ the design-noise-negligible regime and $\rho\to1$ the regime in
which survey noise dominates the between-country spread. (The generative dial in the
simulations is the unbounded design/transport ratio $\mathrm{SD}(\xi)/\mathrm{SD}(R)$;
all reported $\rho$ values, gates, and the $\sqrt{1-\rho^2}$ width law use the bounded
design/total definition above.)

\paragraph{Target and validity.} We want a simultaneous band $\widehat B(r,t)$ that
covers a \emph{new} country's \emph{latent} trajectory,
$\Pr\big(F_{\text{new},r}(t)\in\widehat B(r,t)\ \forall r,t\big)\ge 1-\alpha$.
Two facts frame everything. Ordinary clustered conformal on the observed scores is
exact for the \emph{survey-estimate} target $\Ftilde_{\text{new}}$ (it only needs
exchangeability of countries); its coverage of the \emph{latent} target differs by an
explicit gap that vanishes with the design-noise scale
(Theorem~\ref{thm:oracle}(b)---symmetry of the design law alone is \emph{not} enough
for conservativeness, so we bound the gap rather than assume its sign). Meanwhile its
width is inflated by the design noise, by a factor that grows with $\rho$. Correcting
the width requires estimating and removing the design contribution---a deconvolution.

\paragraph{Country exchangeability, stated honestly.} The transport band's identifying
assumption is exchangeability of countries, and we state what that means: a
superpopulation postulate that the participating countries' trajectory laws are
symmetric draws---over a set that is not sampled, whose members enter and exit rounds
non-randomly, and whose within-country designs change across rounds. It is a strong
idealization (and not comparable to, much less weaker than, the i.i.d.-respondents
assumption, which operates at a different layer inside an inspectable design). Three
things temper it. First, the headline certifications of
Section~\ref{sec:political} do not use it: they are within-country design-bootstrap
statements in which the country effect cancels. Second, when exchangeability holds
only approximately, the nonexchangeable conformal bound of
\citet{barber2023conformal} controls the transport band's coverage gap by a weighted
total-variation distance between country laws; we do not observe that distance, so we
treat the bound as qualitative and flag a leave-one-region-out calibration audit for
the archived replication rather than claiming graceful degradation as fact. Third,
the conservative (Bonferroni-across-countries) rung protects against the family-wise
multiplicity of screening many countries---a distinct safeguard that does not repair
non-exchangeability.

\paragraph{The impossibility.} The central obstruction is that the deconvolution is
not identified from the contaminated scores alone.

\begin{theorem}[Non-identification and necessity of design information]
\label{thm:impossible}
Let the observed score be $\widetilde R = R + \xi$ with $R\perp\xi$. (i)~The law of
$R$ is not identified from the law of $\widetilde R$ without restrictions on the law
of $\xi$: there exist distinct pairs $(\mathcal L_R,\mathcal L_\xi)\ne
(\mathcal L_R',\mathcal L_\xi')$ with $\mathcal L_R * \mathcal L_\xi = \mathcal L_{R'}
* \mathcal L_{\xi'}$. (ii)~Consequently, among non-randomized bands that are
measurable functions of the observed scores only, any band valid for the latent
target on a class of design laws containing $\xi\equiv0$ must be at least as wide as
the plug-in band whose radius is the $(1-\alpha)$ conformal order statistic of
$|\widetilde R|$, up to the conformal discreteness slack. No observed-score band can
beat the plug-in by more than that slack without shrinking the class of admissible
noise laws.
\end{theorem}

The proof (Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}, with a numerical contract test) is a
convolution-invertibility argument for (i) and a conformal converse for (ii). The reading
is constructive: a band both honest and narrower than the plug-in must \emph{supply} the
design-noise law, which complex surveys do provide---through replicate weights or a design
bootstrap, exactly what Section~\ref{sec:method} uses---so this input is not optional.

\begin{remark}[Beyond surveys]\label{rem:general}
Nothing in Theorem~\ref{thm:impossible} is specific to surveys: it holds whenever a
conformal procedure is calibrated on \emph{estimated} objects, so that the nonconformity
score is a noisy version $\widetilde R=R+\xi$ of a latent score---model-based small-area
(MRP) outputs, learned or imputed features, meta-analytic effects. The same two-part
consequence follows: design/measurement information is the identifying ingredient, and its
reliability at a finite number of calibration units is itself bounded
(Proposition~\ref{prop:unreach}). The barrier is confirmed outside surveys on a
model-based small-area calibration (Supplementary Material).
\end{remark}
